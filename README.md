# End-to-End Supply Chain Intelligence for Performance, Profitability and Risk Analytics
## Executive Summary
This project analysed over 180,000 global supply chain transactions spanning three years to evaluate whether commercial demand is being converted into reliable operational execution and sustainable profitability.

Across the period reviewed, the company generated $36.45M in sales, processed more than 64,000 orders, and produced approximately $4M in profit, resulting in an 11% profit margin. The business is commercially active, but the dashboard reveals a major execution gap: 55% of deliveries were late, compared with only 45% delivered on time.

The analysis shows that the main issue is not demand generation. The business is selling and producing profit. The challenge is fulfillment reliability. Late delivery has become the dominant delivery outcome, creating operational risk across regions, shipping modes, customer segments, and high-value products.

The Power BI dashboard was built to help management identify where delivery failures are concentrated, which parts of the business generate the most value, and which operational decisions should be prioritized to improve service reliability and protect profitability.

## Business Problem

The organization has strong sales activity but lacks consistent delivery execution. Over the three-year period, the business generated $36.45M in sales from 64K orders, but more than half of shipments were delivered late.

This creates a clear management problem:

#### The company is generating demand, but its supply chain is not reliably fulfilling that demand on time.

A 55% late-delivery rate means service failure is not occasional. It is the normal operating pattern. This exposes the business to customer dissatisfaction, additional recovery costs, margin pressure, and risk to future growth.

The problem is also not evenly distributed. The dashboard shows that late-delivery risk is concentrated in specific regions, including US Center, West Africa, West of USA, West Asia, and Western Europe. These regions require management attention because they are repeatedly above acceptable delivery-risk levels.

At the same time, profit is concentrated in key areas. The Consumer segment generates the largest profit contribution, and a small group of products accounts for a meaningful share of profitability. This means delivery failures may have a larger business impact when they affect high-profit customers and products.

## Analytical Findings
### 1. Delivery reliability is the biggest operational weakness

The most important finding is that late deliveries exceed on-time deliveries.

Late Delivery Rate: 55%
On-Time Delivery Rate: 45%

This means the company is more likely to deliver late than on time. For management, this points to a fulfillment system that is not operating at the reliability level needed to support growth.

### 2. Revenue is strong, but margin is exposed

The business generated:

$36.45M in sales
$4M in profit
11% profit margin
64K orders

These numbers show that the business has commercial traction. However, an 11% margin leaves limited room for repeated operational inefficiencies. Late deliveries can increase costs through rework, customer service escalation, expedited shipping, and reduced customer confidence.

### 3. Delivery risk is concentrated by region

The dashboard identifies several regions with the highest late-delivery rates:

US Center
West Africa
West of USA
West Asia
Western Europe

These regions are not just minor outliers. They represent recurring delivery-risk areas. Management should treat them as priority zones for operational diagnosis.

### 4. Shipping mode performance is uneven

Shipping modes do not perform equally. Some modes deliver more reliably than others, which means delivery performance can potentially be improved by changing how orders are routed, prioritized, or assigned to shipping methods.

This finding matters because management may not need to solve every supply chain problem at once. A better shipping allocation strategy could improve reliability faster than broad, unfocused operational changes.

### 5. Profit is concentrated in key customer and product groups

The Consumer segment contributes the highest profit among customer segments. Top products also account for a large share of profit compared with the rest of the product base.

This means operational decisions should not treat all orders equally. High-profit customers and high-profit products should receive stronger fulfillment protection because failures in those areas have greater business impact.

### 6. Delivery performance is unstable over time

Monthly delivery trends show repeated periods where late delivery rises above the average. This indicates that the supply chain is not only underperforming overall but also inconsistent month to month.

For leadership, this means delivery performance needs active monitoring, not occasional review.

## Strategic Recommendations
### 1. Make delivery reliability an executive KPI

Delivery performance should be reviewed alongside sales, profit, and margin. A business generating $36.45M in sales cannot treat a 55% late-delivery rate as a back-office issue.

### 2. Prioritize the highest-risk regions first

Management should focus improvement efforts on US Center, West Africa, West of USA, West Asia, and Western Europe before expanding to lower-risk regions.

#### Recommended action:

i. Review fulfillment lead times by region

ii. Identify warehouse, carrier, routing, or capacity issues

iii. Set region-level delivery improvement targets

iv. Track each region against the enterprise late-delivery average

### 3. Reassess shipping mode strategy

Since shipping modes perform differently, management should evaluate which modes are driving reliability and which are creating delivery risk.

#### Recommended action:

i. Compare on-time delivery by shipping mode

ii. Shift priority or high-margin orders toward more reliable shipping modes

iii. Review lower-performing modes for carrier, cost, or process issues

##### Decision influenced: Carrier strategy, shipping policy, and service-level design.

### 4. Protect high-profit customers and products

The Consumer segment and top-profit products should receive stronger operational protection because they contribute the greatest economic value.

#### Recommended action:

i. Prioritize inventory availability for high-profit products

ii. Monitor delivery performance for Consumer orders

iii. Apply stricter service controls to high-margin product categories

iv. Avoid capacity decisions that expose the most profitable demand to avoidable service failures

##### Decision influenced: Inventory prioritization, fulfillment planning, and customer service strategy.

## Business Value Delivered

This solution transforms transactional supply chain data into an executive decision-support platform that connects financial performance, delivery reliability, operational risk, and customer profitability within a single analytical view.

Rather than reporting historical activity, the dashboard identifies where operational performance is constraining business value, helping leadership prioritize interventions that improve delivery reliability, protect profitability, and strengthen operational performance.
